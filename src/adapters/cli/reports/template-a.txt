Serverless API with RDS (template-a)
Workload: Lunch rush, 20 s at 2,000 requests/s (lunch-rush), 60 s, seed 42
Events processed: 730,593

Verdict
=======
Failing. 28,841 of 65,963 answered requests failed (43.7%); 11 were still in progress when the run ended.

Findings
========
1. Failure: 28,841 requests failed with orders-db: too-many-connections, from 14 s to 47 s.
   - That's 23,183 Get order and 5,658 Create order requests, 43.7% of the 65,963 answered.
   - The most in one second was 1,216, in the second ending at 32 s.
   - orders-db held all 90 of its connections in every second with these failures.
   - orders-db refused 28,841 attempts, and each one failed a request.
   - The last failure was in the second ending at 47 s, and none followed in the 13 s after it.
   - After it, orders-db's connections peaked at 29 of 90.
   Fixes:
   - In Wind Tunnel: Raise queryWorkers, so queries stop waiting for a worker. A waiting query holds its connection, so the connections run out only while the workers are full: Template A's lunch rush fails nothing at 64. (Observed: src/adapters/templates/scenarios/lunch-rush.test.ts, "fails requests in every second of the peak"; fidelity RDS-2, RDS-3)
   - In Wind Tunnel: Raising maxConnections alone moves the failure upstream: in Template A's lunch rush at 5,000, waiting queries hold all of fn-orders' concurrency, and Lambda throttles instead. (Observed: src/adapters/templates/scenarios/lunch-rush.test.ts, "fails requests in every second of the peak"; fidelity RDS-2)
   - In AWS: Move to a larger DB instance class, with more CPU and memory. That more CPU serves proportionally more queries a second is an assumption. (Documented: DB instance classes, https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonRDS/latest/UserGuide/Concepts.DBInstanceClass.html; fidelity RDS-2)
   - In AWS: Move reads to read replicas, which scale out beyond one instance for read-heavy workloads. They replicate asynchronously, so a read may be stale. Not modeled. (Documented: Working with read replicas, https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonRDS/latest/UserGuide/USER_ReadRepl.html; fidelity RDS-6)
   - In AWS: RDS Proxy alone isn't a fix: it makes a request wait for a pooled connection, up to ConnectionBorrowTimeout (120 s by default), instead of refusing it, but adds no query capacity. Not modeled. (Documented: RDS Proxy connection settings, https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonRDS/latest/UserGuide/rds-proxy-connections.html; fidelity RDS-6)
   Learn more: docs/learning/aws-did-you-know.md#rds-connection-limits-depend-on-instance-size
   1.1. Note: Requests waited for orders-db's queryWorkers, holding its connections while they waited.
        - All 16 of its queryWorkers were busy in every second from 11 s to 49 s, so requests arriving then waited for one. They were also all busy in 1 other second.
        - Each waiting request holds one of its connections: 16 running and up to 74 waiting is 90, and its connections peaked at 90 of 90.
        - Its connections ran out in 33 seconds from 14 s to 47 s, 3 s after the waiting began at 11 s.
        - While its connections were out, orders-db finished 896 requests a second on average, against 1,770 arriving.
        Cause began at 11 s.

arrivals = ok + failed + rejected + unfinished. "unfinished" work was still running or waiting when the run ended.
"timeouts" isn't part of that sum: the caller stopped waiting, but the component still finished the work as ok or failed.
Latencies are in ms (bucket upper bounds, up to 5% high), of ok work. "busy" is work running; "queue" is work waiting.
Queues: "ok" counts messages accepted. Each is then delivered (async ok), waiting in the backlog, or being delivered.
"async failed" counts failed deliveries; those messages go back to the backlog and are tried again.

"peak" is the most used at once during the run, against the limit in "of": slots held, or tokens drawn out of a full bucket.
"peak %" rounds down. "exhausted" means the limit ran out: every slot held, or less than one token in the bucket, so a request arriving then waited or was rejected (see "peak queue" and "rejected" in Components).
A rate limit's bucket refills at a steady rate. "busiest flow" is the busiest second's tokens taken, against that rate: above it, the bucket drains.
